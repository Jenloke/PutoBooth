Shoot a photo strip together, from two devices

Each of you fills one half of every photo. The countdown runs on both screens at once, and you both get the finished strip. Be in the same room, or on opposite sides of the world.

Start a shared booth

How to start

  1. One of you opens the booth, taps the people icon at the top, then Start a shared booth. You get a code like K7M-4QD, a QR code and an invite link.
  2. The other person scans the QR code, opens the link, or taps Shoot together and types the code.
  3. The preview splits in two: your camera in your half, the other person's live video in theirs.
  4. Either of you presses the shutter. The countdown, flash and photos happen on both devices together.
  5. Each of you styles and saves your own copy. Tap a photo to retake it, together.

What you need

  • Two devices with a camera, in any mix: phones, tablets and computers.
  • Safari or Chrome. If the invite opens inside Messenger, Instagram or another app, open it in your browser: their built-in browsers often block the camera.
  • Any internet connection. You don't need to be on the same Wi-Fi.
  • No account and no app to install.

Private by design

Photos and live video go straight from one device to the other, encrypted. A free connection service (PeerJS) introduces the two devices with a few small messages and never sees your photos. When a phone network blocks a direct connection, relay servers pass the still-encrypted traffic along. Nothing is stored on a server. More about privacy.

Questions

Do we need to be on the same Wi-Fi?

No. Any connection works, mobile data included.

Can one of us use a laptop?

Yes. Any mix of phones, tablets and computers works, as long as each has a camera.

Who gets the photos?

Both of you. Each device keeps its own photos at full resolution and receives the other person's, then saves its own copy with its own style.

How in sync is it?

The devices measure the difference between their clocks and run the same timeline, so the photos usually land within a few hundredths of a second of each other.

What if the connection drops?

The booth notices within about ten seconds and reconnects on its own for up to two minutes. A countdown in progress is cancelled on both devices, so nobody ends up with half a strip.
